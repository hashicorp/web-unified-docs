# Batch job queue

When many batch jobs wait for resources, Nomad can schedule them in any order,
and one tenant's jobs can crowd out other tenants. A Nomad batch job queue holds
a node pool's batch jobs and sends them to the scheduler one at a time, in first
in, first out (FIFO) order or by a dynamic priority that gives each tenant a fair
share of resources. Learn how the queue orders jobs and what tradeoffs it
introduces.

To configure a queue on a node pool, refer to [Queue batch jobs](/nomad/docs/govern/queue-batch-jobs).

## Why use a batch job queue

Batch jobs run until they complete and then stop. Operators often submit more
batch jobs than a cluster has resources for and let Nomad work through them.
When a cluster does not have enough resources, Nomad marks the evaluations for
those jobs as blocked. When resources become available on a node, Nomad
unblocks every evaluation that could run on that node, schedules as many as it
can, and blocks the rest again.

Nomad processes many evaluations in parallel, which makes scheduling fast but
means Nomad cannot guarantee the order in which it schedules jobs. When you
register job A and then job B, Nomad might schedule job B first. When a cluster
has more resources than its jobs reserve, the order does not matter. When many
batch jobs wait for resources, the order becomes unpredictable.

Job priority and resource quotas are also the only fairness controls. In a
cluster that many teams or customers share, one tenant can submit so many batch
jobs that other tenants wait a long time for their jobs to run, even when those
tenants submitted only a few jobs.

A batch job queue addresses both problems. Instead of releasing every batch job
to the scheduler at once, the queue decides which job the scheduler receives
next, which makes the scheduling order predictable.

## How a batch job queue works

You configure a batch job queue on a node pool with the [`batch_queue`
block](/nomad/docs/other-specifications/node-pool#batch_queue-parameters) of the
node pool specification. Each node pool has its own queue, and node pools without
a `batch_queue` block do not queue batch jobs.

When you register a batch job that runs in a node pool with a queue, Nomad adds
the job's registration evaluation to the queue instead of sending it to the
scheduler. The queue then works through its jobs one at a time.

1. The queue selects the next job and sends its evaluation to the scheduler.
1. The queue waits until Nomad places the job's allocations. If the job does not
   fit on the available nodes, the scheduler creates a blocked evaluation, and
   the queue waits for that evaluation to complete as well.
1. After Nomad places the job, the queue selects the next job.

The queue waits for Nomad to place each job, not for the job to finish running.

While a job waits in the queue, `nomad job status` reports the job's status as
`pending`, and the Nomad UI shows the job with the Pending status.

The following behaviors also apply.

- Only batch jobs use the queue. Nomad schedules service, system, and sysbatch
  jobs as usual.
- Only evaluations that job registration creates enter the queue. Nomad sends
  other evaluations for a job, such as the blocked evaluations that the
  scheduler creates, directly to the scheduler. Child jobs that a
  [periodic](/nomad/docs/job-specification/periodic) job launches also skip the
  queue.
- You cannot modify the built-in `default` and `all` node pools, so you cannot
  configure a queue on them. Jobs that run in the `all` node pool never use a
  queue.
- The Nomad leader holds the queue in memory. When leadership changes, the new
  leader rebuilds each queue from the batch jobs in its state store that Nomad
  has not placed yet and from their evaluations.

### Workload status

The [`nomad queue jobs`](/nomad/commands/queue/jobs) command reports each job in
a queue with one of the following statuses.

- `queued` means the job is waiting in the queue.
- `placing` means the queue sent the job to the scheduler and is waiting for
  Nomad to place its allocations, which includes waiting for resources to become
  available.

### Updated and stopped jobs

When you register a new version of a job while an earlier version is still in
the queue, the queue keeps only the newer version. Nomad cancels the evaluation
for the older version and sets its status description to `redundant job-register
eval exists on batch queue`.

The queue treats the newer version as a new job. In a FIFO queue, the newer
version moves to the back of the queue instead of keeping the position of the
older version. In a dynamic priority queue, the age adjustment of the newer
version starts over.

When you stop a job that is waiting in the queue, Nomad removes the job from the
queue and cancels its registration evaluation.

## Queue types

A node pool's queue uses one of two types. The type decides which job the queue
sends to the scheduler next.

| Queue type | Order | Configuration |
| --- | --- | --- |
| FIFO | The order in which Nomad created the jobs' registration evaluations | None |
| Dynamic priority | The highest adjusted priority first, calculated from each tenant's share of allocated resources and the job's age and size | Tenant model, weights, and a recalculation interval |

### FIFO

A FIFO queue sends jobs to the scheduler in the order that you registered them.
It has no configuration options. Use a FIFO queue when you want batch jobs in a
node pool to run in submission order.

### Dynamic priority

A dynamic priority queue calculates an adjusted priority for each job and sends
the job with the highest adjusted priority to the scheduler first. When two jobs
have the same adjusted priority, the queue sends the job it received first.

The adjusted priority exists only inside the queue. It does not change the value
of the job's `priority` parameter.

#### Tenants

A tenant is a group of jobs that the queue tracks allocated resources for, such
as a team or a customer. You choose how the queue assigns jobs to tenants with
the `tenant_type` parameter of the [`tenant_fairshare`
block](/nomad/docs/other-specifications/node-pool#tenant_fairshare-parameters).

- `namespace` makes each namespace a tenant.
- `metadata` makes each distinct value of a job
  [`meta`](/nomad/docs/job-specification/meta) key a tenant. You name the key
  in `metadata_key`.

When you use `metadata` tenants, jobs that do not set the metadata key do not
enter the queue. Nomad sends their evaluations directly to the scheduler.

#### Adjusted priority

The queue calculates a job's adjusted priority as the job's
[`priority`](/nomad/docs/job-specification/job#priority) plus four adjustments.

```plaintext
adjusted priority = job priority
                  + fairshare adjustment
                  + age adjustment
                  + CPU adjustment
                  + memory adjustment
```

The queue truncates each adjustment to a whole number. An adjustment whose
weight is `0` or unset adds nothing.

- The **fairshare adjustment** is the sum of a CPU part and a memory part. The
  CPU part is `(1 - tenant CPU / total CPU) × cpu_weight`, and the memory part
  is `(1 - tenant memory / total memory) × memory_weight`. Both weights are
  parameters of the `tenant_fairshare` block. A tenant whose jobs hold a smaller
  share of the allocated resources than other tenants gets a larger adjustment.
  When no tenant has allocations, the fairshare adjustment is `0`. Refer to
  [Tenant fairshare](#tenant-fairshare) for how the queue measures allocated
  resources.
- The **age adjustment** is `min(1, time since registration / max) × weight`,
  using the parameters of the `age` block. The adjustment grows while the job
  waits and reaches its full weight at `max`.
- The **CPU adjustment** is `(1 - min(1, job CPU / cpu_max)) × cpu_weight`,
  using the parameters of the `job_size` block. Jobs that request less CPU get a
  larger adjustment. Job CPU is the sum of each task's `cpu` resource multiplied
  by its group's `count`. A task that sets
  [`cores`](/nomad/docs/job-specification/resources#cores) instead of `cpu` adds
  `0` MHz to the sum.
- The **memory adjustment** is `(1 - min(1, job memory / memory_max)) ×
  memory_weight`, using the parameters of the `job_size` block. Jobs that
  request less memory get a larger adjustment. Job memory is the sum of each
  task's `memory` resource multiplied by its group's `count`.

For example, consider a queue with the following configuration.

```hcl
dynamic_priority {
  calc_interval = "10s"

  tenant_fairshare {
    tenant_type   = "namespace"
    cpu_weight    = 25
    memory_weight = 25
  }

  age {
    weight = 10
    max    = "1h"
  }

  job_size {
    cpu_weight    = 10
    cpu_max       = 1000
    memory_weight = 10
    memory_max    = 1024
  }
}
```

Two tenants each submit jobs with priority `50` that request 600 MHz of CPU and
256 MB of memory. The CPU adjustment is `(1 - 600 / 1000) × 10 = 4`, and the
memory adjustment is `(1 - 256 / 1024) × 10`, which truncates to `7`. The jobs
waited less than six minutes, so the age adjustment truncates to `0`. After
Nomad places a job for `team-a`, `team-a` holds all of the allocated CPU and
memory, so its next job gets a fairshare adjustment of `0`. The `team-b` jobs
get the full fairshare adjustment of `25 + 25 = 50`, so they move ahead of the
remaining `team-a` job.

```plaintext
JobID        Tenant  Status   Adjusted Priority  Base Priority  Position  Fairshare  Age  Cpu  Memory
team-a-job2  team-a  placing  61                 50             0         0          0    4    7
team-b-job1  team-b  queued   111                50             1         50         0    4    7
team-b-job2  team-b  queued   111                50             2         50         0    4    7
team-b-job3  team-b  queued   111                50             3         50         0    4    7
team-a-job3  team-a  queued   61                 50             4         0          0    4    7
```

#### Tenant fairshare

Tenant fairshare measures the resources that Nomad allocated to each tenant's
batch jobs in the node pool. For each tenant, the queue adds up the CPU in MHz
and the memory in MB of every allocation that belongs to the tenant's batch
jobs. The queue counts allocations in any client status, including allocations
that completed.

To leave allocations out of the calculation, list their client statuses in the
[`exclude_alloc_statuses`](/nomad/docs/other-specifications/node-pool#exclude_alloc_statuses)
parameter. For example, `exclude_alloc_statuses = ["complete", "failed"]` leaves
out the allocations that completed or failed.

The queue calculates a job's adjusted priority when the job enters the queue.
Every `calc_interval`, the queue measures each tenant's allocated resources again
and recalculates the priority of every queued job.

Use the [`nomad queue status`](/nomad/commands/queue/status) command to view
each tenant's allocated resources.

## Tradeoffs

Consider the following tradeoffs before you configure a batch job queue.

- **Serialized scheduling.** The queue sends one job at a time to the scheduler
  and waits for Nomad to place it. Batch jobs in a node pool with a queue
  schedule more slowly than batch jobs in a node pool without one.
- **Head of line blocking.** A job that waits for resources stays at the front of
  the queue and holds every job behind it. The queue does not skip the job to
  run a smaller job that fits. Stop the job to release the queue.

  For a job that Nomad cannot place because of its constraints, such as a
  constraint that no node in the node pool meets, the queue waits only for the
  [`constraint_timeout`](/nomad/docs/other-specifications/node-pool#constraint_timeout)
  duration. The queue treats a job as blocked by constraints when constraints
  filter out nodes and no node runs out of resources, or when the node pool has
  no available nodes. After the timeout, the queue sends the next job to the
  scheduler, and the blocked job waits for placement outside the queue. If you
  do not set a timeout, the job holds the queue until Nomad places it or you
  stop it.

## Monitor a queue

Use the following commands and API endpoints to inspect a batch job queue.

- The [`nomad queue jobs`](/nomad/commands/queue/jobs) command and the
  [`/v1/queue/jobs`](/nomad/api-docs/queue#list-queued-jobs) endpoint list
  the jobs in a queue with their status and position.
- The [`nomad queue status`](/nomad/commands/queue/status) command and the
  [`/v1/queue/status`](/nomad/api-docs/queue#read-queue-status) endpoint
  report the status of a queue, such as the tenants in a dynamic priority queue
  and their allocated resources.
- The `nomad.queue.jobs` and `nomad.queue.watcher.since_job_submitted`
  [server metrics](/nomad/docs/reference/metrics#server-metrics) report the
  number of jobs in each queue and how long the queue has waited for its
  current job to place since you submitted it.
