# Adopt the practice

One or two sentences naming the practice and the outcome it produces. WAF pages
open by establishing why the practice matters before describing it.

## Prerequisites

Optional, and rare in WAF — it appears on one page in 123. Include it only when
the reader genuinely cannot proceed without something specific.

## Why this matters

Explain the problem the practice addresses and the cost of not adopting it. WAF
pages carry this explanatory weight in the body rather than deferring it to a
separate concept page.

## First part of the practice

Body headings are imperative and describe what the reader does, but WAF does not
number them as steps and does not use a requirements block. Keep that shape:
imperative headings, unnumbered, each self-contained.

```shell-session
$ command --flag value
```

## Second part of the practice

Where the guidance differs by product or by maturity level, say so in prose. WAF
uses no tab components anywhere.

## HashiCorp resources

Links sideways, to WAF pages and product documentation covering related guidance.
Write a clause on each link saying what the reader gets from it:

- [Page title](/well-architected-framework/PILLAR/TOPIC) to `<do the related
  thing>`
- [Product page title](/PRODUCT/docs/PAGE) to `<do the related thing>`

### External resources

Optional, and nested under HashiCorp resources rather than given its own H2.
Links to material outside HashiCorp — vendor documentation, standards bodies,
industry writing — that supports the guidance on this page:

- Vendor's [article title](https://example.com) provides additional insight into
  `<the topic>`.

## Next steps

Comes last, and links forward — usually to external resources, tutorials, and
product pages that continue the reader's work:

- Review [resource name](https://example.com) for more information about
  `<topic>`
